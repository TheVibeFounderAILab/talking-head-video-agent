# Edit the idea before the decoration

1. Transcribe everything before selecting. Find the strongest complete thought.
2. Make the opening understandable without the conversation that came before it.
3. Keep the setup needed to make the claim honest. Do not cut qualifiers to make
   a claim more dramatic. Cleaned-up prose is not necessarily spoken footage.
4. Preserve natural phrasing. Cut a repeated premise or tangent, not every breath.
5. Use a dominant take when it holds attention. There is no required cut count.
6. Use screen recordings only when they prove a process or outcome.
7. Keep the speaker's natural exposure. Inspect color metadata before handling HDR.
8. Place captions shot by shot. A coordinate inside a safe box can still cover a face.
9. Listen and inspect the exported video. Preview checks can miss broken animations,
   clipped audio or dropped media. Full decoding and representative frames both matter.
10. Make the final timeline clock match the media duration exactly. A preview that
    can seek but cannot play is unfinished, even if snapshot checks pass.

These are editorial practices distilled for the workshop. They do not include
private examples, personal circumstances or unsupported engagement statistics.
